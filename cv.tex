\section*{Job Market Paper}
``\href{https://zacpond.github.io/pond_jmp.pdf}{The Welfare Effects of Providing Information about Externalities}.''
\Abstract{This paper combines theory and experiments to study the welfare effects of providing information about personal carbon emissions. Governments and agencies routinely use information to motivate prosocial behavior. The welfare effects of doing so depend not only on how behavior changes, but also on how information affects the people who receive it: by improving their decisions, or by producing guilt, pride, or shame. I develop a model with altruism, social-image, and self-image utility that decomposes the total welfare effect of an information intervention into: the instrumental value of the information, its direct effect on image utility, and the external damages avoided through induced abatement. I then estimate these components in experiments with about 6,500 U.S. adults for three interventions: private information on the dollar damages from one's emissions, private information on one's emissions rank, and public recognition, which discloses one's emissions to another participant. I measure consumer welfare effects with an incentive-compatible strategy-method elicitation of willingness to pay to receive or avoid each intervention. I estimate abatement effects both structurally and as randomized treatment effects on contributions to emissions offsets. Private information raises consumer welfare but does not detectably change abatement. Public recognition increases abatement, but decreases consumer welfare and total welfare. Evaluating these interventions by behavior alone would reverse their ranking.}
